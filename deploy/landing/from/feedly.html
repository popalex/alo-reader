Moving from Feedly

Your feeds and folders move in one file. It takes a couple of minutes.

The short version. Download your feeds from feedly.com/i/opml, create a free Alo Reader account, click Subscribe and choose the file. Your feeds arrive in their folders, up to 300 of them.

Export your feeds from Feedly

  1. Sign in to Feedly in a web browser. The export does not work from Feedly's phone app.
  2. Open feedly.com/i/opml. Your browser downloads a file ending in .opml.

If that address ever stops working, open the gear next to Feeds in Feedly's sidebar. The export is next to the Import OPML button.

Import it into Alo Reader

  1. Create an account. It is free: sign up with your email, no card.
  2. Click Subscribe, then Choose OPML file, and pick the file you downloaded.
  3. A short report says how many feeds came in, how many you already had, and which ones failed and why.

Articles appear as each feed is fetched, so a long list takes a few minutes to fill in.

What comes across

  • Every feed that has an RSS or Atom address.
  • Your folders. Each feed lands in the folder it had in Feedly.
  • Up to 300 feeds. If the file holds more, the first 300 come in and the report lists the rest.

What stays behind

An OPML file lists feeds and nothing else, so these stay in Feedly:

  • Read Later articles and Boards.
  • Feedly AI Feeds and Reddit feeds. Feedly leaves them out of the export.
  • Which articles you have read.

What is different here

  • Every article from every feed, newest first. No priority ranking and no AI summaries.
  • The keys you know: j and k move between articles, m marks one read, v opens the original, and Shift+A marks everything read. ? shows the rest.
  • No app store. Alo Reader runs in the browser, you can add it to your home screen, and articles you have opened stay readable offline.
  • Free. There is no paid plan, so nothing here is held back for one.
