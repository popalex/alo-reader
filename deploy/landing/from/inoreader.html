Moving from Inoreader

Your feeds and folders move in one file. It takes a couple of minutes.

The short version. Export your subscriptions from Inoreader's preferences as an OPML file, create a free alo reader account, click Subscribe and choose the file. Your feeds arrive in their folders, up to 300 of them.

Export your feeds from Inoreader

  1. Sign in to Inoreader in a web browser.
  2. Open Preferences, then Import/Export.
  3. Export your subscriptions as OPML. Your browser downloads a file ending in .opml.

Inoreader has renamed that section before, as "Import, export, backup". If the name has changed again, look for where it manages import and export.

Import it into alo reader

  1. Create an account. It is free: an email address and a password, no card.
  2. Click Subscribe, then Choose OPML file, and pick the file you downloaded.
  3. A short report says how many feeds came in, how many you already had, and which ones failed and why.

Articles appear as each feed is fetched, so a long list takes a few minutes to fill in.

What comes across

  • Every feed that has an RSS or Atom address.
  • Your folders. Each feed lands in the folder it had in Inoreader.
  • Up to 300 feeds. If the file holds more, the first 300 come in and the report lists the rest.

What stays behind

An OPML file lists feeds and nothing else, so these stay in Inoreader:

  • Starred and saved articles, and your tags.
  • Rules, filters and monitoring feeds.
  • Which articles you have read.

Newsletter feeds and web feeds are made by Inoreader for you. If they are in the file, they keep working only as long as Inoreader keeps serving them.

What is different here

  • Every article from every feed, newest first. No priority ranking and no AI summaries.
  • The whole app runs from the keyboard: j and k move between articles, as they do in Inoreader. ? shows the rest.
  • No app store. alo reader runs in the browser, you can add it to your home screen, and articles you have opened stay readable offline.
  • Free. There is no paid plan, so nothing here is held back for one.
