Terms and privacy

One page, because you should be able to read all of it.

The short version. This service stores your email address, the feeds you subscribe to, and which articles you have read or starred. It runs no analytics and shows no advertising. Server logs keep request lines, including your IP address, long enough to block anyone abusing the service — never your searches, which are stripped out everywhere. Delete your account and your data goes with it. It is free while in beta and comes with no guarantees.

What is stored about you

Your email address So you can sign in and be contacted about the service.
Your subscriptions and folders The feeds you added and how you organised them.
Read and starred state Which articles you have read, and which you kept.
API tokens you create Stored hashed, and only if you create one.

Article text itself is fetched once and shared between everyone subscribed to a feed. It is not personal data and is not tied to you beyond your read and starred state.

Server logs

Requests are logged: the time, the method, the path, the status, and the IP address they came from. That last one is there for one reason — an account or an address that floods the service has to be identifiable before it can be blocked — and it is the only place your address is kept at all. Entries rotate out within roughly thirty megabytes per service, which on a quiet instance is days and on a busy one is hours; nothing sweeps them into long-term storage.

What those lines do not contain is the query string. That is where a search puts its terms, so it is emptied before anything is written, and the same is done in the three other places a URL travels: the application's own request log, the traces the optional telemetry stack collects, and any error report. All four write [scrubbed] in place of what you typed.

What is not stored

  • Your searches stay out of every log. A search puts its terms in a URL, and a URL ends up in more places than you would expect: the request log, the error log, the traces the optional telemetry stack collects, and any error report. All four replace the query with [scrubbed] before anything is written or sent. Request lines rotate out of the container's log within a few megabytes; where the telemetry stack runs, they are kept for {{if env "LOKI_RETENTION"}}{{env "LOKI_RETENTION"}}{{else}}72h{{end}}.
  • No analytics and no tracking pixels. There is no third-party analytics script on any page, and nothing to opt out of.
  • No advertising, and no profiling. Nothing here builds a profile of your interests or sells anything derived from your reading.

Nobody buys this data, because it is not for sale and never will be. There is no ad network and no data broker in this stack.

One thing worth knowing about images

Articles often contain images hosted by the publisher. Your browser loads those directly from their servers, which means the publisher can see your IP address, exactly as if you had visited their site. Proxying images through this service would prevent that and is on the list, but it is not built yet, and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise.

Fetching the feeds themselves is different: the server polls them on your behalf, so a publisher sees this service's address and its user agent, not yours.

How long things are kept

  • Starred articles are kept indefinitely.
  • Unread articles are never purged.
  • Articles you have read and not starred are removed after {{if env "RETENTION_HORIZON_DAYS"}}{{env "RETENTION_HORIZON_DAYS"}}{{else}}90{{end}} days, and only once every subscriber to that feed has read them too. The period is set per instance; the figure here is the one this instance runs.
  • Database backups are kept for {{if env "BACKUP_RETENTION_DAYS"}}{{env "BACKUP_RETENTION_DAYS"}}{{else}}14{{end}} days. Deleting your account removes it from the live database, but a copy persists in those backups until they age out.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account yourself. Deletion happens at the sign-in provider first, which then notifies this service, and the local cascade runs when that notification arrives — normally within seconds, and the notification is retried for about a day if this service is unreachable. The cascade takes subscriptions, folders, read and starred state, and API tokens with it; there is a test in the repository that fills every one of those tables and then asserts they are empty afterwards. Feeds themselves survive if somebody else still subscribes to them; if nobody does, the feed is removed after a grace period on the next maintenance sweep.

You can take your data out at any time without deleting anything: export your subscriptions as OPML, or read everything through the API with a token you create.

Using the service

  • One account per person, and a limit of 300 feeds per account.
  • Do not point it at things it should not fetch, and do not use it as a backend for another service or as a general-purpose crawler.
  • An account that abuses the poller, the API or other people can be suspended without notice. That is the whole acceptable-use policy.
